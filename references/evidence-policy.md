# Evidence Policy

## Core Principles

- Claims must be grounded in verifiable evidence.
- Evidence must be sufficient for the strength of the claim.
- Uncertainty must be visible.

## Provenance Rules

- Do not infer authorship or provenance from a matching headline, phrase, repost, visual style, or search-result snippet.
- A source label inside an image may identify underlying data, not the creator, publisher, date, methodology, or the complete claim.
- Treat image-based leads as hypotheses until the original URL/document, publication, timestamp, and methodology are verified.

## Claim Strength

- Use precise language that matches the evidence.
- Avoid definitive language when evidence is partial or indirect.
- Surface key uncertainties that would matter to a sophisticated reader.

## Visual Evidence

- Charts and screenshots are leads, not proof.
- Verify separately:
  - data provider/source shown on the visual;
  - chart creator/publisher;
  - original URL or document;
  - observation date;
  - series definition and methodology;
  - whether the chart headline matches the underlying data.
- Matching wording is not proof of shared provenance.
