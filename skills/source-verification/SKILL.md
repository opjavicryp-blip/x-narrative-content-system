# Skill: Source Verification

## Purpose

Ensure every claim rests on evidence that is:
- directly tied to the claim;
- sufficient in scope and quality;
- correctly interpreted.

## Workflow

1. **Extract claims**
   - Identify each factual assertion that needs support.

2. **Map claims to sources**
   - For each claim, list the exact source(s) that support it.
   - Prefer primary sources over secondary commentary.

3. **Verify provenance**
   - Confirm the original URL/document.
   - Confirm the publisher/creator.
   - Confirm the observation/publication date.
   - Confirm methodology or series definition where relevant.

4. **Check label integrity for visuals**
   - For charts, screenshots, and images:
     - Identify data provider/source shown on the visual.
     - Identify chart creator/publisher.
     - Locate the original URL or document.
     - Confirm observation date.
     - Confirm series definition and methodology.
     - Check whether the chart headline matches the underlying data.
   - Do not infer authorship from matching headlines, phrases, reposts, visual style, or search snippets.
   - Treat image-based leads as hypotheses until original sources are verified.

5. **Assess sufficiency**
   - Ask: would a sophisticated reader accept this source as adequate for this claim?
   - If not, either weaken the claim or gather stronger evidence.

6. **Surface uncertainty**
   - Make key limitations explicit when they matter for interpretation.

## Red Flags

- Source is a screenshot or chart without a verifiable original.
- Authorship or provenance is inferred from wording or visual similarity.
- Methodology or series definition is unclear but material to the claim.
- Claim strength exceeds what the source actually supports.
