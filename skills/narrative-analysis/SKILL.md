# Narrative Analysis Skill

## Objective

Identify, track, and analyze market narratives to understand sentiment, positioning, and potential inflection points.

## What Is a Market Narrative?

A narrative is a **shared explanation** for why prices are moving or what will drive future returns.

Examples:
- "ETF inflows are driving Bitcoin higher"
- "Fed pivot is coming, bonds are signaling recession"
- "AI capex cycle will lift semis for years"
- "China stimulus will boost commodities"

## Narrative Lifecycle

1. **Emergence** - New explanation gains traction (1-3 influencers)
2. **Amplification** - Major accounts adopt, media picks up
3. **Consensus** - Becomes dominant explanation, priced in
4. **Exhaustion** - Narrative fails to explain new data
5. **Reversal** - Competing narrative takes over

## Narrative Mapping Framework

### Step 1: Identify Competing Narratives

For any price action, list 2-4 explanations:

**Example: Bitcoin rally in September 2026**

| Narrative | Proponents | Evidence Cited |
|-----------|------------|----------------|
| ETF flows | @APompliano, @WClementeIII | IBIT daily inflows, GBTC outflows slowing |
| Macro liquidity | @LynAldenContact, @RaoulGMI | M2 growth, DXY weakening, real yields falling |
| Technical breakout | @Pentosh1, @benjamin__cowen | Break above $65K resistance, momentum |
| Miner capitulation ending | @williamfoxpel, @HsakaTrades | Hash rate stabilizing, miner reserves rebuilding |

### Step 2: Assess Narrative Strength

Score each narrative 1-5 on:

- **Evidence quality**: Primary data vs. speculation
- **Adoption breadth**: How many credible voices endorse
- **Explanatory power**: Does it explain recent price action
- **Freshness**: New insight or recycled old thesis
- **Falsifiability**: Can it be proven wrong (good narratives can be)

### Step 3: Track Narrative Momentum

Monitor weekly:

- Which narrative is gaining/losing mentions on X
- Media headlines shifting focus
- Analyst upgrades/downgrades aligning with narrative
- Price action confirming or contradicting narrative

### Step 4: Identify Inflection Points

Watch for:

- **Data surprises** that break narrative (e.g., ETF outflows when inflows expected)
- **Credible voices** switching narratives
- **Price divergence** from narrative prediction
- **New catalysts** that old narrative doesn't explain

## Contrarian Analysis

### Find the Consensus

1. Scan top 10-20 analyst accounts on your topic
2. Note which explanation appears most frequently
3. Check media headlines for dominant framing
4. Look at positioning data (if available): COT, fund flows, surveys

### Ask: What If Consensus Is Wrong?

- What data would disprove the consensus?
- What's the alternative explanation nobody is discussing?
- Where is positioning crowded (everyone on one side)?
- What catalyst could flip the narrative?

### Build Contrarian Case

Structure:
1. State consensus view clearly
2. Show 2-3 pieces of contradictory evidence
3. Propose alternative explanation
4. Identify near-term catalyst that could validate your view

## Common Narrative Errors

- **Single-cause fallacy**: Attributing complex moves to one factor
- **Recency bias**: Overweighting latest explanation
- **Hindsight storytelling**: Making past moves seem obvious
- **Narrative lag**: Using old narrative after data changed
- **Echo chamber**: Only tracking voices you agree with
